How EminentTracker scores a lead out of 100

Every parcel in EminentTracker has a score out of 100. It answers one question: of all the land near planned projects, where is a taking most likely and most worth early attention? The score is built from four parts, and the app shows each part beside the total so you can see why a lead ranks where it does.

Need for private land: up to 60 points

The type of work matters most. A new road or a realignment has to be built on land the agency does not own. A widening takes strips of frontage. A bridge replaced on its existing footprint often needs little or nothing. Each kind of project is rated for how often it requires acquisition, and that rating supplies up to 60 of the 100 points.

Timing: up to 15 points

The best moment to reach an owner is when right-of-way acquisition is under way: the plans show which parcels are affected, and appraisals and offers are going out. A project at that point scores the full 15. One in design scores 12, because the plans are coming. One still in planning or environmental review scores little, because there is nothing yet for an owner to act on and the route can change. A project already bid or under construction scores nothing: the land has been acquired. Where an agency publishes no status or dates, the stage is shown as not published and scores low.

Size of the project: up to 10 points

Larger budgets generally mean more land and more owners. Projects are banded by published cost.

Where the parcel sits: up to 15 points

A parcel named in the source document, or one that touches the mapped route, receives the full 15. A parcel that is merely nearby receives 5.

What a score is not

A score is an estimate from public records, produced by automated analysis. It is not a finding that a property will be taken, and a high score on a planning-stage project describes a possibility, not an event. Routes change. Verify the project's status and the ownership record at the source before acting on any lead.
