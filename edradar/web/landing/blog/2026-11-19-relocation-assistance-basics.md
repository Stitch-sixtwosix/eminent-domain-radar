title: Relocation assistance: the claim that runs beside compensation
description: Owners and tenants displaced by a public project may qualify for relocation payments that are separate from the price of the land. A general outline for counsel.
date: 2026-11-19
---
When a public project displaces a household or a business, two separate questions arise. One is what the agency must pay for the property. The other is what help the occupants receive in moving. The second is relocation assistance, and it is easy to overlook because it follows its own rules, its own paperwork and its own timetable.

## A separate system

Just compensation is a constitutional requirement and is ultimately decided by a court if the parties cannot agree. Relocation assistance is a creature of statute and regulation. Federal law sets relocation requirements for projects that use federal money, and most states have their own provisions for projects that do not. The programme is normally administered by the acquiring agency, through a relocation agent, and disagreements usually go through an administrative review before any court is involved.

The practical consequence is that settling the price of the land does not necessarily settle relocation, and the reverse is also true. Confirm early which rules govern the project, because that depends on who is funding it and which agency is acquiring.

## Who may qualify

Eligibility generally turns on occupancy, not ownership. A tenant who owns nothing may be a displaced person, and an owner who does not occupy the property may have little or no relocation claim. Residential occupants, businesses, farms and non-profit organisations are commonly covered, each under different provisions. The date on which occupancy is measured matters, and the rules on that point should be checked for the project in question.

## What the assistance generally covers

The details vary by programme, but the usual categories are:

- **Advisory services.** Help in finding a replacement site, and information about the process and the payments available.
- **Moving costs.** Either documented actual expenses or a fixed payment chosen in place of them.
- **Replacement housing.** For residential occupants, a payment directed at the difference between what they had and the cost of a comparable replacement dwelling, with separate treatment for owners and tenants.
- **Business re-establishment.** For businesses, certain expenses of setting up at a new site, usually subject to limits.

Many programmes also provide that a residential occupant should not be required to move until comparable replacement housing has been made available, and that occupants receive written notice a minimum period before they must vacate.

## What it usually does not cover

Relocation payments are not a substitute for business damages. Lost profits and loss of goodwill are generally outside the relocation programme. Whether they can be recovered at all, in the condemnation case or elsewhere, is a question of state law, and the states answer it very differently. An owner who assumes the relocation agent's figures cover everything the business will lose should be told otherwise early.

## Where claims go wrong

Most problems in relocation claims are procedural, not legal. Common ones are:

- **Moving too soon.** An occupant who leaves before receiving the agency's notice of eligibility may put the claim at risk.
- **Missing records.** Payments for actual expenses depend on estimates, invoices and receipts. A move organised without them is hard to document afterwards.
- **Missed time limits.** Claims must be filed within set periods, and so must requests for review of a denial.
- **Committing before approval.** Some expenses need to be approved by the agency before they are incurred.
- **Overlap.** An item paid as relocation generally cannot be paid again as compensation, so the two claims need to be reconciled.

## What this means at intake

Ask who occupies the property, on what terms and since when. Ask whether anyone has heard from a relocation agent and what was said or signed. Tell occupants not to move, sign a lease elsewhere or dispose of equipment on the strength of an informal conversation. If the firm handles only the valuation side, say so in writing and make sure the client knows who is watching the relocation deadlines.
