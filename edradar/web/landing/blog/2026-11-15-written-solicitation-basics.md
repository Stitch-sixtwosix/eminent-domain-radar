---
title: Writing to owners before a taking: what to check before you mail
description: Lawyers may generally write to prospective clients, but the rules on how are specific. A checklist of the points to confirm in your own state before sending.
date: 2026-11-15
---
Contacting a property owner about a planned public project is lawyer advertising, and in most states a targeted letter is treated as a solicitation. It is generally permitted. It is also regulated in detail, and the details differ from state to state.

This is a list of what to confirm, not a statement of any one state's rule.

## Written contact is usually allowed; live contact usually is not

Most states follow the structure of ABA Model Rule 7.3: a lawyer may send a truthful written communication to someone known to need legal services, but may not solicit by live telephone call or in person when a significant motive is the lawyer's own gain. Check how your state treats email, text messages and voicemail, because they are not treated uniformly.

## Labelling

Many states require the words "Advertising Material" or "Advertisement" on the outside of the envelope or the face of a postcard and at the start of the communication. Some set a minimum type size or a position. Confirm the exact wording your state requires.

## Truthfulness

Rule 7.1 forbids false or misleading statements. For a project still in planning, that has a specific meaning: do not say or imply that the owner's property will be taken. Say what the public record shows, that no taking has been announced, and that it may never happen.

## How you learned of the matter

Some states require the letter to disclose how the lawyer obtained the recipient's name or learned of their situation. Stating that the information came from public planning and property records is good practice everywhere.

## Filing and record keeping

A number of states require a copy of each solicitation to be filed with the bar or kept for a set period, with a list of the people it was sent to. Know the period and keep the list.

## Stop when asked

If a recipient says they do not want to hear from you, do not contact them again. Record the request so that nobody else at the firm does either.

## Email adds a federal layer

Commercial email is also covered by the CAN-SPAM Act: an accurate sender and subject line, a postal address, a clear way to opt out, and an opt-out honored within ten business days.

*This article is general information, not legal advice. Check the current rules of every state where you are licensed and where your recipients live.*
